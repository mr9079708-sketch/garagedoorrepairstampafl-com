The basics

What Is Emergency Garage Door Repair?

Emergency garage door repair is a priority call-out for a door that is unsafe, unsecured, or trapping a vehicle. It covers situations such as a snapped spring, a door off its tracks, a cable that has let go, or an opener that has failed with the door open. You need it when waiting until morning would leave your home exposed or your car stuck inside. The technician first makes the door safe, then repairs it or secures it until parts are available.

What's included

What Our Emergency Garage Door Repair Includes

Every emergency call includes a full diagnostic, the repair itself, and a safety test before we leave. Here is what is covered on a standard visit, and what we quote separately if your door needs it.

01

Full Diagnostic Inspection

We check springs, cables, rollers, tracks, hinges, and the opener before quoting. You get a plain explanation of the failure and the fix. This takes about 15 minutes and costs nothing if you approve the repair.

02

Broken Spring Replacement

We replace torsion or extension springs with matched pairs rated for your door weight. Springs are balanced to within a pound and tested through several cycles. Standard replacement runs $250 to $450 installed.

03

Cable and Drum Repair

Snapped galvanized steel cables are replaced in pairs, and the cable drums are rewound and inspected. We also check the bottom brackets and hinges while the door is apart. Most cable jobs finish in about an hour.

04

Track and Roller Realignment

Bent tracks are straightened or replaced, and worn rollers are swapped for nylon garage door rollers. We realign the vertical and horizontal runs so the door travels smooth. This is often the fix for a door that shudders or binds.

05

Opener and Sensor Repair

We repair or replace opener gears, logic boards, and photo eye sensors. Remotes and wall mount keypads are reprogrammed and tested. If the opener is beyond repair, we quote a replacement before doing anything.

06

Safety Test and Walkthrough

Every job ends with a safety reverse test and sensor check per UL 325. We cycle the door at least three times and confirm it closes flush. Then we show you what failed and how to keep it running.

Tools & methods

Tools and Methods We Use

Purpose-built equipment, used by trained technicians, so the job is done accurately the first time.

01

Garage door torsion spring winding bars

Used to safely release and set tension on torsion springs. The wrong bar can slip and cause serious injury, so we use matched steel bars sized to the spring.

02

Garage door cable drums and winding cones

Cable drums hold the lift cables, and winding cones lock the spring assembly. We replace worn drums and cones whenever we replace cables to keep the system balanced.

03

Garage door roller removal tool

Lets us swap rollers without bending the track or hinge. It speeds up a full roller replacement to under an hour and keeps the door aligned.

04

Garage door opener remote tester

Confirms whether a remote is transmitting and whether the opener receiver is picking up the signal. It saves guesswork when a remote stops working.

05

Garage door sensor alignment tool

Aligns photo eye sensors precisely so the safety reverse works every time. A misaligned sensor is the most common reason a door will not close.

06

Garage door track bender

Straightens minor bends in the track without replacing the section. For deeper bends we replace the track, but this tool handles the small stuff on site.

07

Garage door torque wrench

Used to tighten hinge and bracket bolts to spec. Over-tightening strips threads; under-tightening lets hardware rattle loose over time.

What to Look for Before You Book

Before you book any garage door company, a few simple checks can save you from a poor job and a surprise bill.

  • Ask for the licence number before work starts, even on an urgent call, and check that it matches the name on the quote.
  • Get the price in writing before work starts, and ask whether there is an out-of-hours charge.
  • Describe the problem clearly on the phone, including the position of the door, so the right parts are sent.
  • Check recent reviews for comments about response time and pricing on urgent calls.
  • Ask what warranty covers the parts and the work, and for how long, and get it in writing.
  • Check that the technician arrives in a marked vehicle and can say who they are and which company sent them.
