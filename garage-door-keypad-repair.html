The basics

What Is Garage Door Keypad Repair?

Garage door keypad repair fixes the wireless keypad mounted outside the garage that opens the door with a PIN code. Keypads stop working when the battery dies, the buttons wear out, the code is lost, or the unit loses its connection to the opener. You need it when the keypad does not respond, accepts the code only sometimes, or has stopped working after a storm or battery change. Repair usually means testing the battery and signal, reprogramming the code, and replacing the unit if the electronics have failed.

Problems we solve

Keypad Problems We Fix Every Week

Most keypad calls fall into a handful of patterns. Here's what we see across Tampa and how we handle each one.

ISSUE

Keypad lights up but the door won't move

We test the keypad's signal and the opener's receiver separately. Usually the keypad lost its pairing or the receiver is failing. We re-pair the keypad or repair the receiver, then confirm the door cycles three times before we leave.

SYMPTOM

Keypad won't accept a new code

We reset the keypad to factory settings, clear stale codes and program fresh PINs for everyone in the house. If the board won't take a reset, we replace the keypad with a new wireless unit and pair it to your opener.

SIGN

Keypad is completely dead, no lights

We check the battery first, then the wired terminals at the opener. Corroded contacts from Florida humidity are the usual cause. We clean or replace the contacts, install a fresh battery and test the keypad from the driveway.

ALERT

Keypad works for some people, not others

We test each button individually. If the pad is worn, we replace the membrane or the keypad. If the buttons are fine, we check the opener's memory for stored remotes and clear out old entries that can interfere.

Pricing process

Keypad Repair Pricing in Tampa

$95 - $260 depending on repair vs. replacement and wiring needs

Phase 01

Keypad type and brand

A basic Chamberlain or Genie wireless keypad costs less to replace than a LiftMaster MyQ smart keypad. Wired keypads that need new low-voltage wire add labor time and a small materials cost.

Phase 02

Repair vs. replacement

Cleaning contacts, replacing a battery and reprogramming is the cheapest outcome. Replacing a dead board or the whole keypad costs more, but it's still far less than an opener replacement.

Phase 03

Wiring condition

If the original low-voltage wire is corroded or undersized, we re-terminate or re-run it. That adds time but prevents the new keypad from dropping codes in the same way the old one did.

What to Look for Before You Book

Before you book any garage door company, a few simple checks can save you from a poor job and a surprise bill.

  • Ask for the licence number before work starts, and check that it matches the name on the quote.
  • Read recent reviews and look for comments about punctuality, pricing, and follow-up, not just the star rating.
  • Get the price in writing before work starts, including parts and labour, and ask what happens if more work is found once the door is open.
  • Ask what warranty covers the parts and the work, and for how long, and get it in writing.
  • Check that the technician arrives in a marked vehicle and can say who they are and which company sent them.
  • Ask whether the price includes a safety test and a walkthrough at the end, so you can see the door working before they leave.

Why is my garage door keypad not working in Tampa?

In Tampa, the most common cause is a dead battery or corroded battery contacts from humidity, followed by a keypad that lost its programming after a power surge. If the keypad lights up but the door never moves, the issue is usually the wireless pairing or the opener's receiver, not the keypad itself. We test all three in one visit and fix the actual cause instead of replacing parts until something works.

How much does garage door keypad repair cost in Tampa?

Most keypad repairs in Tampa run $95 to $185 in 2026, including the service call and labor. A battery and reprogram sits at the low end. If the keypad board is dead and we install a new wireless keypad, expect $150 to $260 depending on the brand. We quote the price before we start, so you're never guessing.

How do I reset my garage door keypad in Tampa?

For most LiftMaster and Chamberlain keypads, enter the existing PIN, press and hold the program button until the light blinks, then enter a new 4-digit code and press enter. Genie and Linear units use a different sequence. If the keypad won't accept the reset, the board or battery is usually the culprit, and that's when a garage door keypad programming Tampa service call saves you an afternoon of trial and error.

Who repairs garage door keypads in Tampa?

We do. Our techs repair and replace keypads for every major brand, including LiftMaster, Chamberlain, Genie, Craftsman, Linear and Marantec, across Tampa and Hillsborough County. How do I change the code on my garage door keypad in Tampa?

Most keypads let you set a new code by entering the old code, pressing the program or learn button, and entering the new code, but the exact steps depend on the brand and model. If you have lost the old code, the keypad usually needs to be cleared and re-paired with the opener, which the technician can do on a repair visit. Change the code whenever someone who had it no longer needs access, such as a former tenant or contractor.

Why does my garage door keypad work sometimes and not others in Tampa?

The most common cause is a weak battery, which can still power the keypad sometimes but not reliably. Worn buttons, a loose connection, or interference can also make it intermittent. Try a fresh battery first, since that fixes many keypads. If it still works only sometimes, the keypad itself is probably failing and should be tested by a technician.

Can a garage door keypad be replaced without replacing the opener in Tampa?

Yes, in most cases. A new wireless keypad can be paired to your existing opener as long as the opener is a compatible brand and the receiver still works. The technician programs the new keypad, sets your code, and tests it several times before leaving. If the opener itself is very old, the technician will tell you whether a compatible keypad is still available for it.
