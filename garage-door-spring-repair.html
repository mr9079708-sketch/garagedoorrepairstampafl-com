The basics

What Is Garage Door Spring Repair?

The springs are what make a heavy door feel light. Torsion and extension springs carry the weight, so the opener only guides the door and a person can lift it by hand. When one lets go, the door turns into dead weight, the opener strains, and the whole system is unsafe to force. A bang from the garage, a door that rises only a few inches, or a door that suddenly feels far heavier by hand all point at the springs. Wound steel holds a large amount of stored energy, which is why the fix belongs to a trained technician with proper winding tools rather than a weekend DIY attempt.

About this service

What are the warning signs of a failing garage door spring in Tampa?

The door gets heavier to lift by hand, opens unevenly, or stops a foot off the ground. You may hear creaking or popping from above the door, or see a gap in the spring coil where it has cracked. Rust on the spring is another warning sign. If you notice any of these, do not keep running the opener, since it can burn out trying to lift a door the springs no longer support.

How do I know if my garage door spring is torsion or extension?

Look above the door with it closed. A torsion system has a horizontal bar with one or two coiled springs on it. An extension system has long springs running parallel to the horizontal tracks on either side, with a pulley and cable at each end. Torsion is the standard on most modern doors, while extension is common on older and lighter doors. The technician will confirm the type before quoting, because the parts and the method are different.

Can a garage door work with one broken spring?

A door with two springs can sometimes be lifted with one broken, but it should not be used that way. The remaining spring is carrying twice its designed load, the door will be heavy and uneven, and the opener will strain to lift it, which can burn out the motor. The second spring has done the same number of cycles and is close to failing too. Both springs should be replaced together, and the door should not be used until they are.

Why did my garage door spring break in the middle of the night?

Springs usually break when the door is not moving, often at night when the temperature drops. The steel contracts as it cools, and a spring that is already weakened by rust or fatigue lets go at the weak point. A loud bang in the night is the classic sign of a broken spring. Do not try to open the door in the morning, because it will be very heavy, and running the opener against it can burn out the motor. Call a technician.

Know your parts

Torsion vs Extension: Which Spring System Your Tampa Door Uses

Most Tampa garage doors use one of two spring systems, and knowing which you have explains how the door behaves and what the repair involves. Torsion springs sit on a bar above the door, and they work by twisting as the door closes, storing energy that lifts the door when it opens. They are the standard on most modern doors, they are smoother and more balanced, and they last longer. Extension springs run along the tracks on either side of the door, and they work by stretching as the door closes. They are common on older and lighter doors, and they are simpler, but they are less balanced and they need safety cables.

You can tell which you have by looking above the door with it closed. A torsion system has a horizontal bar with one or two coiled springs on it. An extension system has long springs running parallel to the horizontal tracks, with a pulley and cable at each end. The repair is different for each: torsion springs are wound with winding bars and the tension is set by counting turns, while extension springs are replaced with the door clamped and the cables secured. The technician identifies the system before quoting, because the parts and the method are different.

  • Torsion springs: mounted on a bar above the door, smoother, more balanced, and longer lasting.
  • Extension springs: run along the tracks, common on older and lighter doors, and need safety cables.
  • How to tell: a bar with coiled springs above the door means torsion; long springs along the tracks mean extension.
  • Different repair: torsion springs are wound with winding bars; extension springs are replaced with the door clamped.
  • Safety cables: extension springs must have safety cables running through them, and many older doors do not.

How long things last

How long do garage door springs last in Tampa?

Most torsion springs are rated for about 10,000 open-and-close cycles, which works out to roughly 7 to 10 years for a typical household, and less for a door that is used many times a day. Tampa humidity can rust springs faster if they are never lubricated. A spring that is rusty, stretched, or has a visible gap has already failed or is close to it, and it should be checked by a technician rather than left to snap.

Should both garage door springs be replaced at the same time in Tampa?

Yes, in most cases. The two springs on a door have done the same number of cycles, so when one breaks the other is usually close behind. Replacing both keeps the door balanced and means you are not paying for a second visit a few weeks later. The technician will show you the broken spring and explain the condition of the other one before you decide.

Tampa climate

Why Tampa Springs Fail Faster Than Springs Up North

A torsion spring rated for 10,000 cycles should last the same anywhere, but in practice Tampa springs fail sooner, and the reasons are all climate. The first is humidity, which rusts the steel. A spring that is never lubricated will develop rust on the coils, and rust creates weak points where the steel cracks under tension. The second is heat. A west-facing garage can reach very high temperatures in summer, and the repeated heating and cooling cycles fatigue the steel faster than a steady temperature would.

Usage is the third factor. Many Tampa homes use the garage as the main entrance, so the door cycles four or more times a day, and a 10,000-cycle spring reaches its limit in seven years instead of ten. Salt air near the bay adds a fourth factor, speeding up corrosion on any exposed steel. The defense is simple and cheap: lubricate the springs twice a year with a silicone-based lubricant, keep the garage as dry as you can, and have the springs checked when the door starts feeling heavier. A spring that is caught early is a planned replacement. A spring that is ignored until it snaps is a bang in the night and a car trapped in the garage.

  • Humidity: rust creates weak points where the steel cracks under tension.
  • Heat: repeated heating and cooling in a hot garage fatigues the steel faster.
  • Usage: a door used as the main entrance can reach 10,000 cycles in seven years, not ten.
  • Salt air: homes near the bay see faster corrosion on exposed steel.
  • Prevention: lubricate twice a year, and book a check when the door starts feeling heavier.
