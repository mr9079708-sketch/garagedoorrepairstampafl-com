What Our Opener Repair Service Includes

Every opener repair visit covers a full diagnostic and the standard labor to get your unit running. Parts are quoted separately at flat prices, and we tell you before we start if the repair is not worth the money compared to a new opener.

01

Full Motor Diagnostic

We test the motor, capacitor, travel limits and force settings with the door disconnected, so we can tell whether the opener itself has failed or the door is dragging it down. You see the results before any part is quoted.

02

Gear and Board Repair

Stripped opener gears and failed logic boards are the two most common internal failures we see. We replace them with parts matched to your LiftMaster, Chamberlain, Genie or Craftsman unit, then run a full cycle to confirm smooth travel.

03

Sensor and Safety Calibration

Photo eye sensors get cleaned, aligned and tested, and we set the auto-reverse to UL 325. A door that reverses for no reason is usually a sensor issue, and it is one of the cheapest fixes on the list.

04

Remote and Keypad Programming

We program your remotes and wall mount keypad to the repaired unit, replace dead batteries, and test range from the driveway. If a remote has failed outright, we can supply a replacement that pairs with your opener.

05

Door Balance and Hardware Check

While we are on the ladder, we check the torsion springs, cables, rollers and hinges for wear that will shorten the life of the repair. Anything we find gets pointed out with a price, and you decide what to do.

Problems we solve

Opener Problems We Fix Across Tampa

Most opener calls fall into a handful of failure patterns. Here are the ones we see daily and how we handle them.

ISSUE

Opener running but not lifting the door

We disconnect the opener and test the door by hand first. If the springs or cables are the cause, we quote that repair instead, which usually costs far less than a new motor. Then we reset the opener force to match the repaired door.

SYMPTOM

Blinking safety sensor stops the door closing

We clean and realign the photo eye sensors, check the wiring and the bracket height, and test the auto-reverse with a 2x4. If a sensor has failed, we replace it and confirm the opener reads it correctly before we leave.

SIGN

Remote or keypad stops working

We test the remote with an opener remote tester, reprogram it to the unit, and check the keypad wiring. If the hardware has failed, we supply a replacement remote or keypad that pairs with your opener and test it from the driveway.

ALERT

Opener is loud, grinding or shaking the door

We inspect the opener gears, rollers, hinges and track, lubricate the moving parts with garage door lubricant, and replace what is worn. Most noisy doors quiet down after a roller and hinge service plus a force adjustment.

Why is my garage door opener not working in Tampa?

The three most common causes in Tampa are a burned-out capacitor, a stripped opener gear, and misaligned photo eye sensors after a storm. Power surges from summer lightning take out logic boards too. We test the motor, gears, sensors and door balance in one visit, so you get the real cause instead of a guess and a parts bill.

How much does garage door opener repair cost in Tampa?

Most repairs run $180 to $450 in 2026, depending on the failed part. A sensor alignment or remote reprogram sits at the low end; a gear and capacitor rebuild or a logic board replacement sits higher. Motor replacement, if the unit is beyond saving, runs $450 to $850 installed. We quote the flat price before we start work.

Can I repair my garage door opener myself in Tampa?

You can safely replace a remote battery, reprogram a keypad, or wipe and realign photo eye sensors yourself. Anything past that is a different story. Torsion springs, cables and opener gears store lethal energy, and UL 325 force settings need a scale and a tester to set correctly. For those, call a licensed tech.
