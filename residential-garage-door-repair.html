Tools & methods

Tools and Methods We Use

Purpose-built equipment, used by trained technicians, so the job is done accurately the first time.

01

Garage door torsion spring winding bars

Used to safely load and release torsion spring tension. Winding a spring without the right bars is how homeowners get hurt, which is why we don't recommend DIY spring work.

02

Garage door cable drums and winding cones

Cable drums hold the lift cables and set the door's balance. Winding cones let us set proper cable tension on torsion systems without over-tightening.

03

Garage door roller removal tool

Lets us swap rollers without bending the track or damaging the hinge. Faster and cleaner than prying rollers out with a screwdriver.

04

Garage door sensor alignment tool

Aligns photo eye sensors precisely so the safety reverse works every cycle. Misaligned sensors are the top cause of a door that won't close.

05

Garage door track bender

Straightens minor track bends without replacing the whole section. For severe bends, we replace the track instead of forcing it back.

06

Garage door torque wrench

Sets hinge and bracket bolts to spec so hardware doesn't vibrate loose. Loose bolts are a common source of rattling and panel sag.

Problems we solve

Common Garage Door Problems We Fix in Tampa

Most Tampa garage door calls fall into a handful of failure patterns. Here's what we see, what happens if you ignore it, and how we fix it.

ISSUE

Broken torsion spring leaves the door too heavy to lift

We replace both torsion springs with matched, oil-tempered pairs rated for your door weight, reset the drum tension, and cycle-test the door. Most spring jobs finish in about 90 minutes.

SYMPTOM

Snapped cable drops one side of the door

We replace both galvanized cables as a set, inspect the cable drums for wear, and check that the door lifts level. If a panel was damaged, we quote the panel repair separately before starting.

SIGN

Opener runs but the door won't move

We open the unit, test the gears and board, and repair or replace what failed. On LiftMaster and Chamberlain units, a gear and sprocket kit often fixes it for a fraction of a new opener.

ALERT

Blinking safety sensor blocks the door from closing

We align both sensors, check the wiring and brackets, clean the lenses, and test the reverse with a 2x4. If a sensor is dead, we replace it and re-pair the system.

Door size and weight

A two-car insulated door needs heavier springs and more labor than a single-car door. Spring cost scales with door weight, and heavier doors take longer to balance and test.

Phase 02

Spring type

Torsion springs cost more than extension springs but last far longer. If your door has extension springs, we'll explain the trade-off before recommending a conversion.

Phase 03

Parts quality

Oil-tempered, cycle-rated springs and galvanized cables cost more than bargain imports but last two to three times longer in Tampa humidity. We quote both when it matters.
