Signs Your Garage Door Spring Is About to Break

A garage door spring rarely fails without warning. Here are the sounds, sagging, and lift problems Tampa homeowners notice one to three weeks before it snaps.

G
garage door repairs Tampa FlOctober 2026 · 10 min read

A garage door spring is about to break when the door suddenly feels heavier than usual, the opener strains or reverses halfway up, you hear a loud bang from the garage, or you spot a visible gap in the coil. Those four signs show up in roughly that order, usually one to three weeks before the spring snaps. In Tampa, where the average garage door cycles 6 to 10 times a day, that window is often shorter than homeowners expect.

Below we walk through what each warning sign looks and sounds like, how long torsion springs actually last in Florida heat, and the mistakes that turn a $180 repair into a $900 one. We have replaced enough springs across Hillsborough County to know which symptoms mean "call today" and which mean "call this week."

What a Garage Door Spring Actually Does

Garage door spring repair in Tampa starts with understanding that the spring, not the opener, lifts your door. A standard 16x7 double-car door weighs 130 to 180 pounds. The torsion spring above the door counterbalances almost all of that weight, so your opener only has to push maybe 10 to 15 pounds.

That is why a failing spring feels like a failing opener. The motor is doing the same work it always did, but the spring has stopped carrying its share. Most of the "my opener is dying" calls we run are actually spring calls.

There are two spring types on Tampa homes:

  • Torsion springs mount on a shaft above the door and twist. Standard on nearly every home built after 1990.
  • Extension springs run along the horizontal tracks on each side. Common on older Seminole Heights and West Tampa bungalows.

Torsion springs are the ones that snap with a bang. Extension springs stretch and fail more quietly, which is why they are usually paired with a safety cable.

Why Does a Garage Door Spring Break in the First Place?

A garage door spring breaks because it has hit its rated cycle count, and Florida heat gets it there faster. Every open-and-close is one cycle. A standard 10,000-cycle spring is rated for about 7 years on a door that runs 4 times a day, or 3 years on a door that runs 10 times a day.

Heat is the multiplier. A garage in Tampa can hit 120°F on an August afternoon. That heat cycles the steel thousands of times a year, and it accelerates corrosion on the coils. Springs on west-facing garages in neighborhoods like Carrollwood and Town 'N' Country tend to go before springs on shaded doors of the same age.

Three other things shorten spring life here:

  1. Salt air within a few miles of the bay, which pits the steel and creates stress risers.
  2. Dry, unlubricated coils that grind against each other instead of sliding.
  3. A door that is out of balance, which forces one spring to carry more than its share.

The Seven Warning Signs We See Most Often

These are the signs our technicians look for on every Tampa spring call, ranked roughly from earliest to latest.

1. The door feels twice as heavy when you lift it by hand

Disconnect the opener and lift the door halfway. A healthy door stays put. A door with a dying spring drops the moment you let go, or takes real effort to raise. If you can feel the weight, the spring has already lost 30 to 50 percent of its tension.

2. The opener strains, grunts, or reverses halfway up

Modern openers have a force sensor. When the spring stops helping, the opener hits its limit and reverses, often blinking a light code. Homeowners replace the opener. The spring was the problem.

3. You hear a loud bang from the garage

A torsion spring breaking sounds like a gunshot inside the garage. It usually happens on the way up, when the spring is under maximum load. If you heard that bang and the door will not open at all, the spring is already gone.

4. A visible gap appears in the coil

Look at the spring above the door. A healthy torsion spring is a tight, even coil. A spring that is about to break often shows a 1/8-inch to 1/4-inch gap where the steel has stretched, or a rust-colored streak where the coil has started to crack.

5. The door opens crooked, with one side lagging

On a two-spring door, one spring usually fails first. The door tilts as it rises, and one cable goes slack. That is a garage door cable repair in Tampa call waiting to happen if the door keeps running off balance.

6. The door slams down instead of easing shut

Springs also cushion the door on the way down. When they weaken, the door drops the last 12 to 18 inches instead of lowering smoothly. That is hard on panels, hinges, and the opener gear.

7. You see metal shavings or rust dust under the spring

Fine gray or orange dust on the floor beneath the torsion tube means the coils are grinding. It is the last stage before a snap.

What a Broken Spring Sounds and Looks Like

A broken garage door spring announces itself with a single loud bang, followed by a door that will not open or opens only a few inches. If you were home when it happened, you probably thought something fell in the garage.

After the bang, you will usually see one of these:

  • A clean break in the torsion spring, with the two halves separated by an inch or more.
  • A dangling extension spring on one side, held only by its safety cable.
  • A door stuck open or stuck closed, with the opener running but the door not moving.

Do not run the opener after a spring breaks. The motor will pull against 150 pounds of dead weight, and it can burn out the gear assembly or snap a cable. Call for emergency garage door repair in Tampa and leave the door alone until we get there.

How Long Do Tampa Garage Door Springs Last?

In Tampa, plan on 5 to 8 years for a standard 10,000-cycle torsion spring, or 10 to 14 years if you upgrade to a 25,000-cycle spring. The difference is about $40 to $70 per spring in parts, and it is the single best upgrade for a door that gets used daily.

Common Mistakes Homeowners Make With a Failing Spring

The most expensive mistake is running the opener after the spring breaks. That turns a spring job into a spring job plus a stripped opener gear, and it can drop the door on whatever is underneath.

Other mistakes we see regularly:

  • Replacing one spring when both are the same age. The second spring fails within a few months, and you pay the labor twice.
  • Buying a spring by door width instead of by wire gauge, inside diameter, and length. A mismatched spring will fail early and can damage the torsion tube.
  • Using the wrong lubricant. White lithium grease attracts grit in a Tampa garage. Use a silicone-based or dedicated garage door lubricant on the coils and bearings.
  • Adjusting the springs yourself. Torsion springs store enough energy to break bones. There is no safe DIY version of this job.
  • Ignoring a bent track or worn roller while the door is apart. A door that runs rough wears out its new springs faster. If you hear grinding, a garage door roller repair in Tampa is worth doing at the same visit.

If your door is more than 15 years old and you have replaced springs twice, the smarter money may go toward a garage door replacement in Tampa rather than a third spring job on a tired door.

Frequently Asked Questions

How do I know if my garage door spring is broken or just weak?

A broken spring shows a visible gap or two separated halves, and the door will not open at all. A weak spring still opens the door but feels heavy by hand and makes the opener strain. If you see a gap, it is broken. If you only feel the weight, it is failing and should be replaced within days.

Can I open my garage door with a broken spring?

No. A door with a broken spring weighs 130 to 180 pounds, and pulling it by hand can drop it on you. Do not run the opener either, since it will burn out the gear assembly. Call for emergency service and leave the door where it is.

How much does garage door spring repair cost in Tampa?

Expect $180 to $280 for a single torsion spring and $280 to $420 for both, parts and labor, at 2026 Tampa pricing. High-cycle springs add $40 to $70 each. Extension spring pairs run $150 to $250. A standard job takes 45 to 90 minutes.

How long does a garage door spring last in Florida heat?

A standard 10,000-cycle spring lasts 5 to 8 years on a Tampa door that cycles 6 to 10 times a day. Upgrading to a 25,000-cycle spring stretches that to 10 to 14 years. West-facing doors and homes near the bay land at the shorter end of those ranges.

Should I replace both springs at the same time?

Yes. Springs installed on the same day wear at the same rate, so the second one usually fails within a few months of the first. Replacing both at once costs $100 to $140 more in parts and saves you a second service call and a second day without a working door.
