Early warning signs

Signs You Need Garage Door Repair in East Lake-Orient Park

Most garage door failures give a warning first. These are the signs we hear about most often, and what each one usually means.

01

Door reverses before it hits the floor

A door that starts down and then reverses is usually a safety sensor out of line, a sensor blocked by dirt or sunlight, or an opener force setting that is too sensitive. Check the sensors for dirt and alignment first, and call us if the door keeps reversing after that.

02

Opener runs but the door does not move

When you hear the motor run and the door stays put, the fault is usually a stripped gear, a broken drive belt or a disconnected trolley. The opener is working, but nothing is connecting it to the door, and running it again only wears the gears further.

03

Door shudders or grinds going up

Shuddering and grinding usually mean worn rollers, a bent track or dry hinges, and the friction wears the springs and opener gears while it goes on. Cleaning the track and fitting a full set of rollers is the usual fix, and it is cheaper than the opener repair that follows if it is left.

04

Door hangs crooked in the opening

A door that sits uneven, with one side higher than the other, usually has a frayed or snapped cable or a worn cable drum. The opener strains against the imbalance, so the door should not be run again until the cables are replaced as a pair and the door is re-levelled.

We wipe the opener's memory and program fresh remotes on the visit, and we can supply extra remotes if the new tenant needs more than one. The second item is the sensors and the balance. A door that has been used hard by a previous tenant may have worn rollers, a tired spring or a sensor that has been knocked out of alignment. We check the sensors, the balance, the springs and the rollers on a changeover visit, and we fix anything that is worn before it becomes a call-out. The third item is the door itself. Look at the bottom of the door for rust, check the panels for dents, and run the door through a full cycle and listen. Grinding means track or roller damage; a loud bang means a spring has let go; a door that hangs crooked has usually lost a cable. Doors That Cycle All Day: Springs, Rollers and Openers Under Load

A garage door in East Lake-Orient Park can cycle ten or twenty times a day, and that changes how every part of it wears. The springs, the rollers and the opener all work harder here than they do on a door that opens twice a day, and the maintenance has to match that.

Springs are the first part to show the load. A torsion spring is rated for a number of cycles, and on a door that cycles all day that number is reached sooner than the owner expects. The signs are easy to spot once you know them: the door feels heavy when you lift it by hand, it drifts down after you let go halfway up, or it opens unevenly with one side higher than the other. A spring that is about to fail sometimes gives a loud bang, and after that the door will not open at all. We replace springs as a pair and size them to the door's actual weight, which we measure on the visit, and on a high-cycle door we fit springs rated for more cycles than the standard, because the extra cost is small and the extra life is large.

Rollers wear in the same pattern. The original rollers on a door that cycles all day wear flat and start to grind within a few years, and a door that shudders or grinds on the way up is usually a roller problem. We fit sealed nylon rollers as standard, which run quieter and last far longer than the steel or plastic rollers they replace, and we check the track for bends and for bolts that have worked loose, because a track that has shifted will wear a new set of rollers out just as fast. A full set of rollers, a track clean and a balance check is a small job that adds years to a high-cycle door's life.

The opener carries the same load, and it shows it in the gears and the circuit board. A light-duty opener fitted to a door that cycles all day will strain every time it runs, and the gears wear out sooner than they should. When we visit we check the opener as well as the door, and we set the force limits to the door rather than leaving them at the factory default. A modern belt-drive opener with a battery backup is a genuine improvement on a high-cycle door: it runs quieter, it handles the weight better, and it keeps working through a power cut. If the old opener is already struggling, replacing it at the same time as the springs is usually better value than paying for two separate visits.

The maintenance that pays on a high-cycle door is regular and simple. Lubricate the hinges, rollers and springs twice a year, and book a tune-up once a year so we can check the balance, the springs and the rollers before they fail. A door that is looked after lasts years longer than one that is left alone until something breaks, and in a community where the doors cycle as often as they do here, that regular check is worth more than it is anywhere else. If you manage rentals, a yearly tune-up for each property is the cheapest insurance you can buy against an emergency call-out.
