Daylight or a gap under the closed door

Salt Air Off the Bay: The Slow Damage to North Hyde Park Doors

North Hyde Park sits close to the bay, and the salt air here is the main reason doors fail early. It does not announce itself. It works on the metal a little at a time, and the first sign is usually a part that breaks without warning.

The parts that suffer most are the ones that carry load. Cables are the classic example: galvanised steel cable looks sound from the outside while it corrodes where it winds onto the drum, and the first warning is often a frayed strand or a cable that snaps when the door is halfway up. Springs are next. The coil surface pits in salt air, and a pitted spring loses strength unevenly, so the door starts to feel heavier on one side and then lets go without warning. Bottom brackets rust where rain splashes up from the driveway, and once a bracket is weakened the cable can pull through it. We check all of these for corrosion on every visit in North Hyde Park, because replacing the failed part without checking its neighbours is how a door breaks again a month later.

Opener electronics suffer too, and in a different way. Humidity gets into the circuit board and the safety sensors, and the result is a door that works one day and not the next, or a remote that only works from a few feet away. Owners often blame the remote battery, and sometimes it is the battery, but if a new battery does not fix it, the receiver or the sensor wiring is the next place to look. We carry replacement receivers and sensors on the truck, and we seal the connections on the new ones so the damp does not get back in. A door that hesitates or reverses for no reason is worth a call: it is usually a sensor problem, and a sensor problem left alone can leave the door stuck open overnight.

The door itself shows salt damage along its bottom edge first. The steel skin blisters and bubbles where the coating has failed, and once the blistering starts it spreads quickly in this air. Washing the door down with fresh water a few times a year slows it down a lot, because it removes the salt film before it can eat into the finish. Touch up any chip that reaches bare metal promptly, and do not ignore a small rust spot at the bottom of the door: it is the visible end of corrosion that is usually worse inside the panel. Where the bottom of a door is already badly rusted, a replacement bottom section or a new door is usually better value than patching, because a patched bottom will rust again within a year or two.

The maintenance that pays in North Hyde Park is simple and regular. Lubricate the hinges, rollers and springs with a proper garage door lubricant twice a year, and use a silicone spray on the seals so they do not stick and tear. Wash the door down after the summer storms, when the salt film is at its worst. And book a tune-up once a year so we can check the balance, the cables and the springs before they fail.
