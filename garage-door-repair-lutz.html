Early warning signs

Signs You Need Garage Door Repair in Lutz

Most garage door failures give a warning first. These are the signs we hear about most often, and what each one usually means.

01

Door slams down instead of closing smoothly

A door that drops fast on the way down has lost spring tension or cable support, and it is dangerous to stand under. Do not try to catch it or force it back up. Secure the area and call us, because a slamming door is a spring or cable failure that needs fixing before the door is used again.

02

Daylight or a gap under the closed door

A gap under a closed door, or daylight showing at the sides, usually means a worn bottom seal, a track out of alignment or a door that is no longer level. It lets in rain, dust and pests, and it usually points to a balance or track problem rather than just a seal.

03

Loud bang from the garage

A loud bang usually means a torsion spring has snapped. The door will feel far too heavy to lift by hand, and forcing it can damage the opener or drop the door crooked. Secure the door, do not try to lift it, and call for a spring replacement.

04

Door reverses before it hits the floor

A door that starts down and then reverses is usually a safety sensor out of line, a sensor blocked by dirt or sunlight, or an opener force setting that is too sensitive. Check the sensors for dirt and alignment first, and call us if the door keeps reversing after that.

If the garage is used for storage, keep the area around the door clear and do not lean anything against the track or the panels. Lutz garage door repair storm

Storm Season on the Suburban Edge: Getting the Door Ready

Lutz sits inland from the bay, but storm season still matters here, and the garage door is the largest opening in most homes on these streets. A door that is ready for a storm is a door that has been checked before the season, not during it.

The first thing to check is the wind rating. Doors fitted in the newer subdivisions are usually built to the current wind-load standards, but the doors on the older homes may predate them, and a door that has been patched and repainted over the decades may be weaker than it looks. The rating label is usually on the inside of the top panel or on the side of the door, and we can read it for you and tell you whether it meets the current standard. If it does not, a replacement before storm season is the sensible time to do it, because a rated door with proper bracing is the single biggest improvement you can make to how the house handles a storm.

The second thing to check is the hardware that holds the door in its tracks. Wind pressure pushes on the whole panel, and the force goes through the rollers, the track and the brackets into the wall. Brackets that have rusted, track bolts that have worked loose, and rollers that are worn flat are all weak points, and they are the parts that fail first when the wind rises. Before storm season, check that the track is bolted tight to the wall and that the brackets are sound. We check all of this on a tune-up, and we replace any bracket or bolt that is corroded rather than leaving it to be tested by the next storm.

Trees are the third problem, and in Lutz they are a bigger problem than in most places. The mature oaks and pines drop limbs in a storm, and a limb that lands on a panel bends the panel and the track behind it. Before storm season, trim back any limb that overhangs the door or the roof, and clear the track and the seal of leaves and debris. It takes an afternoon and it is the cheapest storm preparation you can do. After a storm, look for dents and for limbs on the roof or the door before you run the door, because a door that is run with a bent track will wear its rollers out.

After a storm, the checks are simple and worth doing in order. Look at the door from outside: is it sitting level, or is one side lower than the other? A door that hangs crooked has usually lost a cable or a spring and should not be run again until it is levelled. Then run it once through a full cycle and listen. Grinding means track or roller damage; a loud bang means a spring has let go. Then look at the bottom seal and the tracks for debris and rust. If anything is wrong, secure the door so it cannot fall and call us. We secure the door first, then repair the part that failed, and we test the balance before we leave.
