Early warning signs

Signs You Need Garage Door Repair in University

Most garage door failures give a warning first. These are the signs we hear about most often, and what each one usually means.

01

Remote works sometimes, or not at all

A remote that works from the driveway one day and not the next is usually a weak battery, a failing receiver or a sensor problem, not a dead opener. Try a fresh battery first, and if the door still ignores the remote, we test the receiver and the safety sensors on site.

02

Door slams down instead of closing smoothly

A door that drops fast on the way down has lost spring tension or cable support, and it is dangerous to stand under. Do not try to catch it or force it back up. Secure the area and call us, because a slamming door is a spring or cable failure that needs fixing before the door is used again.

03

Daylight or a gap under the closed door

A gap under a closed door, or daylight showing at the sides, usually means a worn bottom seal, a track out of alignment or a door that is no longer level. It lets in rain, dust and pests, and it usually points to a balance or track problem rather than just a seal.

04

Loud bang from the garage

A loud bang usually means a torsion spring has snapped. The door will feel far too heavy to lift by hand, and forcing it can damage the opener or drop the door crooked. Secure the door, do not try to lift it, and call for a spring replacement.

Rentals, Student Housing and High Turnover Around the University

University seasons

Semester Rhythms, August Move-Ins and Summer Neglect

The University neighborhood runs on the semester calendar, and garage door calls follow it. August brings move-ins, with new tenants, new remotes and doors that have sat unused over the summer, and May brings move-outs, with doors that need a check before the next tenant arrives. A landlord who schedules a service visit between tenants will catch a worn spring, a dead remote battery or a misaligned sensor before it becomes a midnight call from a new tenant, and we work with landlords regularly to keep their doors in shape through the turnover.

Summer is the quiet season for the neighborhood, and it is the hardest season for the door. The students are gone, the houses sit empty or half-empty, and the garage door sits closed and unused, which is hard on a door in its own way: lubricant dries out, seals stick, and a door that has not moved in months can seize when it is finally used. If you are leaving for the summer, a service visit before you go will keep the door in good shape, and a check when you return will catch any problem before it becomes a failure.

The heat is the other summer factor. A garage in the University area in August can be far hotter than the house, and that heat dries out lubricant, ages rubber seals and stresses opener electronics. If your opener starts acting up only on hot afternoons, heat is a likely cause, and moving the opener's logic board or adding ventilation is sometimes part of the fix. The practical habit that helps most is to keep the door maintained on a schedule rather than waiting for it to fail, because in a rental a failed door usually fails at the worst possible hour.

Hurricane season is a consideration here too, and a door that has been neglected all year should be inspected before the season starts. Before June we recommend an inspection that checks the balance, tightens all hardware and looks for rust or rot in the panels and track, and we can talk honestly about bracing or a wind-rated replacement. A door that is already loose or out of balance is the one most likely to fail when the wind picks up, and a rental deserves the same protection as an owner-occupied home.
