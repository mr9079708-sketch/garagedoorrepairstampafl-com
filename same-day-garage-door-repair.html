While you wait

What to Do While You Wait for the Technician

The most important thing while you wait is to stop using the door. A door with a broken spring or a snapped cable is far heavier than it looks, and running the opener against it can burn out the motor or pull the door off its tracks entirely. If the door is stuck halfway, leave it there. Do not try to push it up or pull it down, and do not let children play near it. A half-open door can drop without warning if a second cable or spring lets go.

There are a few safe things you can do. If the opener is humming or grinding, pull the red emergency release cord only if the door is fully closed, so the door is disconnected from the opener and cannot be driven by a faulty motor. If the door is stuck open and you cannot stay home, move valuables out of sight and lock the interior door into the house. Take a short video of the noise the door makes, or a photo of any visible damage, and have it ready when the technician arrives, because it speeds up the diagnosis. And if the door fell or slammed shut, check that nobody was hurt first, then call.

  • Do not force the door: pushing or pulling a damaged door makes the repair bigger and can be dangerous.
  • Stop the opener: unplug it or switch it off if it is straining, grinding, or running without moving the door.
  • Keep clear: keep children and pets out of the garage until the technician has checked the springs and cables.
  • Secure the house: lock the door from the garage into the house, and move valuables out of sight if the garage door is stuck open.
  • Note the symptoms: when it happened, what you heard, and whether the door moved at all helps the technician bring the right parts.

After the fix

After the Same-Day Fix: What We Check Before We Leave

A same-day fix is fast, but it is not rushed, and the checks at the end are the same as on any other repair. The technician starts with the balance: the door is disconnected from the opener and lifted by hand, and it must stay where it is left at the halfway point. A door that is out of balance will wear out its new parts quickly and strain the opener, so the springs are adjusted until the balance is right. Then the door is reconnected and run through several full cycles, listening for grinding, thumping, or hesitation.

Then the safety systems are tested. The photo eye sensors are checked by breaking the beam while the door is closing, and the door must reverse. The auto-reverse is tested with a 2x4 laid flat on the floor, and the door must reverse on contact. The remote and the keypad are tested, the manual release is checked, and the weather seal is checked along the bottom. Only then does the technician show you the old part, explain what was done, and leave. If any test fails, the opener is adjusted before we leave, not after, however late in the day it is.

  • Balance test: the door must stay put at the halfway point when lifted by hand.
  • Full cycles: several complete open-and-close runs, listening for grinding or hesitation.
  • Sensor test: breaking the photo eye beam must reverse a closing door.
  • Auto-reverse test: the door must reverse on contact with a 2x4 laid flat.
  • Walkthrough: the old part is shown to you and the work explained before we leave.
